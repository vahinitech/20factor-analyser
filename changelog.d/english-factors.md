- Every English factor a photo can measure is tested against a mistake planted for it on 20 real writers' letters, each planted page against a clean twin that differs only by the mistake (`scripts/factor_tests.py`, `benchmark.py factors`, a weekly and on-change workflow). Five factors measured something other than their name and now measure the ink: Loop Closure counts the closed bowls against the letters read that have one (open loops caught on 85% of pages, was 0%); Letter Spacing the spread of the gaps between letters (100%, was 25%); Line Straightness the letters' wander off their line's baseline (a wavy line 100%, was 20%); Slant Consistency the spread of the words' lean (100%, was 0%); Baseline Alignment also counts words sitting off their line (a sunken word 100%, was 5%). Size Consistency counts words 30% off the page's usual size, reads a word's height from the columns that reach at least half a small letter, and measures a word sitting off its line from where it sits, so a sunken word is no longer a size change. The older stand-ins remain only for pages too small to measure. English scores move: on the 20 real UJI pages overall falls 2 to 7 points, mostly Letter Spacing and Slant Consistency, which had scored about 9.7 on any page.
