# Source of the pages

**What these pages are, and are not.** Facsimile plates of adult italic
handwriting from one English teachers' book of the 1890s and 1900s: the
writing a pupil was asked to copy, written by the author in her own
hand and printed from her pages (plate 6 was engraved on copper from
her writing). This is the italic hand that the later English joined
italic schemes grew from, not one of those schemes itself. They are the
ideal end of the range, not children's everyday writing.

Only running sentences were cropped: no alphabets, stroke tables,
numerals, signatures or typeset text (the book's introduction is
typeset in italic type and was left out). The crops were cut from the
Internet Archive's page images on 5 November 2026, each kept to the
handwritten block, and scaled down to 2600 pixels wide where wider.
Nothing else was changed.

## M. M. Bridges, A New Handwriting for Teachers (1907 printing)

- Mary Monica Bridges (born Waterhouse), 1863-1949, wife of the poet
  Robert Bridges. Oxford: printed at the University Press by Horace
  Hart; sold by Henry Frowde. The title page reads "Oxford: Printed at
  the University Press 1907" and the back of it "Fourth impression".
  The first edition was printed at Oxford by 1899 (Eton College Library
  holds an 1899 printing).
- Scan: New York Public Library copy, scanned by Google and uploaded to
  the Internet Archive by John Mark Ockerbloom,
  https://archive.org/details/ANewHandwritingForTeachers
- Rights: the item's metadata (https://archive.org/metadata/ANewHandwritingForTeachers)
  has no `possible-copyright-status`, `licenseurl` or `rights` field.
  It records only "date: 1907", "collection: opensource, community" and
  "description: Book scanned by Google from the New York Public Library
  and uploaded to the Internet Archive by John Mark Ockerbloom.
  Downloaded from https://books.google.com/books?id=bf9PAAAAYAAJ".
  The rights rest on the book itself: published in the United Kingdom in
  1907 (fourth impression of a book first printed by 1899), so in the
  public domain in the USA (published before 1931); Bridges died on
  9 November 1949 (Wikipedia, "Monica Bridges"), so it is also in the
  public domain in India (author's life plus 60 years, ended 2009).
  The Google scan adds no copyright of its own; the "Digitized by
  Google" mark at the foot of each page is outside every crop.

| `bridges-008-read-not` | n37 | plate 8 | "Read not to contradict & confute..." (8 lines) |

Plates 7, 8 and 9 are, in the author's words, "true facsimiles" of her
current hand. Plates 10 and 11 (a Latin manuscript of Edward VI's time
and Michelangelo's Italian) are 16th-century hands in other languages
and were left out.

## Style of each page

| Page | Slant | Scheme | Joins and breaks seen |
|---|---|---|---|
| `bridges-006-ways-of-life` | slanted (slight) | Bridges' italic, engraved | Letters stand mostly apart, linked by hairline exit strokes (a, n, t); the engraver's curves are rounder than her pen |
| `bridges-007-stone-pine` | slanted | Bridges' italic, own hand | Joined within most words through exit strokes (under, murmurs, sweet, western); breaks after b (breeze), around f (soft, of), and after descending g, p, y |
| `bridges-008-read-not` | slanted | Bridges' italic, own hand | As plate 7; & and capitals stand apart |
| `bridges-009-bays` | slanted | Bridges' italic, own hand | As plate 7; breaks around f, after b and y |
| `bridges-009-musk-rose` | slanted | Bridges' italic, own hand | As plate 7; breaks around f (flower, first, forth) and x (excellent) |


## Not found with a clear licence

- Modern named schemes (Victorian Modern Cursive, NSW Foundation,
  Queensland and South Australian styles, Nelson, Letterjoin, Getty-Dubay
  cursive italic, California "joined italics"): no adult model page under
  a licence that allows commercial reuse. Searched Wikimedia Commons
  (file namespace) for "italic handwriting", "cursive italic
  handwriting", "Victorian Modern Cursive", "NSW Foundation
  handwriting", "joined handwriting", "Getty-Dubay italic".
  - `File:Cassowarys Victorian Modern Cursive.png` is genuine handwriting
    under CC BY-SA 3.0 ("A sample of my Victorian Modern Cursive
    handwriting, handwritten by User:Cassowary"), but the writer's age is
    not stated, it is not a model copy, and it is only 668 x 223 pixels
    (2 lines). Not used: adult writer unverified.
  - `File:Getty-Dubay Italic sample.svg` is tagged public domain but
    credits Barbara Getty and Inga Dubay, 1976; a 1976 work is not
    public domain by age, and the tag gives no reason. Not used:
    unverified (and an SVG redrawing, not a scan).
- Marion Richardson, Writing and Writing Patterns (1935) and her other
  books: in copyright in the USA until 2030 (published 1935). Not used.
- Alfred Fairbank, A Handwriting Manual (1932), and the italic books of
  the 1930s to 1950s on archive.org (Fairbank, Gourdie, Blunt, Dumpleton):
  published after 1930, so not public domain in the USA. Not used.
- Edward Johnston, Writing & Illuminating, & Lettering (1906): public
  domain (Johnston died 1944), but its italic is a formal book hand, not
  joined everyday handwriting. Not checked page by page; not used.
- UK DfE and Australian state exemplars: none found as adult model text
  under the Open Government Licence or a CC licence; not used.
- The Journal of the Society for Italic Handwriting
  (`pub_journal-of-the-society-for-italic-handwriting` on archive.org):
  the Society was founded in 1952, so every issue is after 1930. Not used.

The written lines of each crop are in `set.json` (`page_text`), lower
case, with the words split across lines as the copy splits them, and
each page's slant and scheme in `page_style`. The pages are committed,
not cut again on each run, for the reason `benchmarks/uji-clean/source.md`
gives.
