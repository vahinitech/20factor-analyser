# Source of the pages

**What these pages are, and are not.** Model copies of upright (vertical)
looped cursive from one American copybook series of 1898: the writing a
pupil was asked to copy, printed from plates above blank practice lines.
They are the ideal end of the range, not children's everyday writing.
The copybook scanned is unused: the practice lines below each copy are
empty, so no pupil's writing is on any page.

Only running sentences were cropped: no drills, single words, alphabets,
signatures or printed text. Copies with dates or numbers were left out,
because the figures in them are set in type, not written. The crops were
cut from the Internet Archive's page images on 5 November 2026, each kept
to the written copy (the printed page number above it is left out), and
scaled down to 2600 pixels wide where wider. Nothing else was changed.

## Barnes's National Vertical Penmanship, No. 6 (1898)

- No author named. New York, Cincinnati, Chicago: American Book Company,
  1898. The book's own notice reads "Copyright, 1898, by American Book
  Company" (scan image n198). The scan binds Nos. A, B and 1 to 6
  together; all pages here are from No. 6.
- Scan: University of California copy, digitised by Google,
  https://archive.org/details/barnesnationalv00unkngoog
- Rights: the Internet Archive item records "possible-copyright-status:
  NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT". Published in the USA in 1898, so in the public
  domain there. No author is named; the copyright is the publisher's,
  and the work was published 128 years ago, so it is also in the public
  domain in India (an anonymous or corporate work, publication plus 60
  years).

## Style tags

All six pages: slant upright. Round looped vertical hand: looped
ascenders on b, f, h, k, l and looped descenders on f, g, j, y. Small
letters are joined all through each word, with no break letters seen.
Most capitals stand apart from the letter after them (C, H, M, N, O,
S, T, Y in these copies); not every capital was checked one by one.

## Searched and not used

- Shaylor, How to Teach Vertical Writing (Ginn, 1898;
  `howtoteachvertic00shay`, "The Library of Congress is unaware of any
  copyright restrictions for this item"; Shaylor 1845-1925). Rights are
  clear, but the written copies are single short lines ("Be of good
  cheer.", "Dare to do right."), not two or more running lines.
- Newlands and Row, Teachers' Manual to the Natural System of Vertical
  Writing (Heath, 1896 and 1907; `teachersmanualto00newliala`,
  `teachersmanualt00newlgoog`, NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT). The written copies are
  one line each with printed captions between them (chart, scan n39),
  and A. F. Newlands' death year was not found, so India rights are
  unverified (R. K. Row died 1932).
- Newlands and Row, Public School Writing Course, Vertical System, No. 5
  (Toronto, 1896; `publicschoolwrit00newl`): no rights field on the item,
  Newlands' dates unverified, and one faint line per page.
- Jackson, Upright versus Sloping Writing (1894;
  `uprightversusslo00jack`): printed text and diagrams only, no written
  copy. Jackson's Theory and Practice of Handwriting
  (`theorypracticeof00jackrich`, NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT) was not used: John
  Jackson's death year was not found, so India rights are unverified.
- Canadian vertical copybooks on Canadiana microfilm (`cihm_92542`,
  `cihm_93933`, Eldon and Casselman 1896) and Casselman, The Public
  School Vertical Copy Book (1899, `publicschoolvert00cass`): no rights
  field on the items, authors' dates not found. Unverified.
- Indian upright cursive: the Internet Archive's Digital Library of
  India copybooks (`dli.ernet.213824` "Daniels Copy Book",
  `in.ernet.dli.2015.417852`, "Sodagar Standard Copy Book" Nos. 1 to 5)
  carry no date, author or rights field, and most are Gujarati. Wikimedia
  Commons searches for "upright cursive", "straight cursive", "Indian
  cursive handwriting" and "vertical handwriting" found no openly
  licensed Indian school cursive specimen. None found with a clear
  licence.
