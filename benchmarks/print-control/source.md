# Source of the pages

**What this page is, and is not.** Adult model "manuscript writing"
from a 1920 British handbook for schools: separate letters written
with a broad pen, not joined. It is the print-only control for the
cursive sets. Only one page qualified; the target was 3 to 6 (see the
end of this file).

Only the running sentence was cropped. The crop keeps the written
block. The figure is drawn inside thin ruled guide lines that show its
margins; the lines touching the writing could not be cut away and stay
in the crop. Three small printed measurement labels (3/4", 1 1/8",
1 3/4") and the printed caption "Fig. 32." were inside or next to the
block; they were painted over with the colour of the paper around them.
Nothing else was changed.

## Manuscript Writing and Lettering: A Handbook for Schools & Colleges (1920)

- "An educational expert" (anonymous). London: Sir Isaac Pitman & Sons,
  1920.
- Scan: University of Alberta Libraries copy,
  https://archive.org/details/manuscriptwritin00educ
- Rights: the Internet Archive item has no `possible-copyright-status`,
  `licenseurl` or `rights` field. Its metadata records "creator: An
  educational expert", "publisher: London, Sir I. Pitman & sons, ltd",
  "date: 1920". Published in 1920, so in the public domain in the USA.
  India: an anonymous work published 60 or more years ago is in the
  public domain. The book does not name the writer of the figure; if the
  author is ever identified and died after 1965, this would change.
  Treat the rights as settled by date and anonymity, with no archive
  field to quote.

## Not found, and what was searched

- Marjorie Wise, "On the Technique of Manuscript Writing" (1924): not
  on the Internet Archive (searched by title and by creator).
- Internet Archive title searches for "manuscript writing", "print
  script", "script writing", penmanship and lettering books of 1910 to
  1930. US manuscript-writing books found were 1931 or later (Voorhis
  1931, Jones 1948, Haines 1945).
- M. M. Bridges, "A New Handwriting for Teachers" (1907; Bridges
  1863-1949), https://archive.org/details/ANewHandwritingForTeachers:
  the writing is a part-joined italic with ligatures, not print, and
  the item has no rights field (a Google scan). Not used.
- Edward Johnston, "Writing & Illuminating & Lettering" (1906): its
  plates are historical manuscripts, not adult model print handwriting.
- Freehand lettering books (Reinhardt 1911, Speedball 1925 and 1927,
  Esterbrook "Drawlet Pens" 1925, Szoeke 1929): sign and draughtsman's
  lettering, not handwriting, and for Reinhardt and Speedball the
  authors' death years were not found (unverified for India). Szoeke
  died in 1969, so his book is still protected in India.
- Wikimedia Commons: searches for manuscript writing, print script,
  printed handwriting and block letters, and the categories Teaching
  scripts and Handwriting practice printables. The print models there
  are vector charts or fonts (`Handwriting Without Tears Print
  sample.svg`, `Hamburger Druckschrift ab 2011.jpg`), not handwriting.

The written lines of each crop are in `set.json` (`page_text`), lower
case, with the words split across lines as the copy splits them, and
each page's slant and scheme in `page_style`. The pages are committed,
not cut again on each run, for the reason `benchmarks/uji-clean/source.md`
gives.
